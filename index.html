Music — Memory

The Childhood iPod, or: What the Past Keeps Trying to Tell You

On plugging in an old device and finding a ten-year-old who already knew everything that mattered.

By the Editor — May 14, 2026

I plugged in an old iPod today and got ambushed. Beyoncé came on first, then Taylor Swift, then a Cabaret cast recording with no business being in a child's music library. Funny, how the things you chose before you had language for yourself end up being the clearest record of who you already were.

Speak Now era Taylor Swift. Dolores O'Riordan. A song about longing and luck and people who want too much. Little me had a lot to say and no one to say it to, so he said it in playlists instead — quietly, like a letter he never sent.

Whispers of Doom

May 14, 2026

In the hollow where the laughter dies,
And shadows stretch from vacant eyes,
A cold caress, a spectral sigh,
The phantom of myself drifts by.

Each tick of time, a rusty chime,
Echoes a forgotten paradigm.
The walls close in, a stony dread,
As cobwebs drape around my head.

A presence breathes upon my neck,
A silent, gnawing, spectral wreck.
It siphons light, it drains the hue,
Leaving behind a chilling dew.

The mirror shows a stranger's gaze,
Lost in a perpetual, murky haze.
A whispered name, a forgotten plea,
Bound to this haunting, misery.

And when the moon, a sliver pale,
Casts lonely patterns on the trail,
I walk with ghosts, a silent host,
Lost to the world, forever lost.

About

the editor & the drawer

[Replace this with your actual about text. Here's a placeholder to get you started:]

Dead Letters is a junk drawer. It holds the things that don't fit anywhere else — book reviews and music notes, photographs taken on odd afternoons, poems that arrived without warning, essays about whatever is currently demanding to be written about.

It is maintained by one person, written by hand (more or less), and published without a schedule or a niche. There are no ads, no algorithms to please, and no particular ambition beyond getting the words out of the drawer and onto the page.

If you're here, welcome. You can stay as long as you like.
